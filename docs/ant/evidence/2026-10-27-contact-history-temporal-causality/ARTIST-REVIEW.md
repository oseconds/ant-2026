# Artist review — contact-history temporal correspondence

## Verbatim responses

Initial response, before the condition labels were explained:

> «“음 view1번이 더 일단 잘 보이긴해 발자국이라고 써진거 보기전에 발자국 같다고 느꼈어 뭔가 가루를 떨어뜨리는거같기도 하고
> view2는 비슷하긴한데 먼가 더 직선같은 느낌 잘 눈에 안보이기더 하고”»

After learning that View 1 was the temporally decoupled B condition:

> «“아 맞아 오히려 점만 생기는걸 포착해서 지나갔던 발자취인건가 해리포터 발자국지도같네 했어”»

Further clarification:

> «“어 저게 맞아 따로 배경지식 없이 보려고했으니까 이게 지나간 오래된 흔적이다 보다는 투명개미도 있나 이런식에 가까웠어”»

The artist corrected an attempt to reduce those associations to a single interpretation:

> «“둘 다 생각한거야
> 따로 하나에 가깝다가 아니라
> 어 이런건가 이렇게도보이네
> 하는 감상”»

Order-effect caveat:

> «“근데 view1을 먼저본 영향도 고려는해야해”»

## Record and limits

- View 1 was noticed more strongly. Before explanatory wording, it spontaneously suggested footstep-like marks and powder/deposition.
- After the mapping was disclosed, the artist described several possible readings: passed traces, invisible-agent footsteps, and a Marauder's-Map-like association. The later clarification says these were multiple openings, not one dominant reading.
- View 2 was described as more line-like and less noticeable.
- View 1 was presented first. The artist explicitly raised first-view/order influence as a confound; this is promising evidence, not a clean preference result.
- The hidden mapping was View 1 = temporally decoupled permutation control B; View 2 = exact immediate causal A.
- Do not treat these associations as proof that the marks read as old/history, retained matter, or a settled material identity.
