# BODY comparison across natural COMMIT scopes

## Result

Five additional scopes reveal broad structural variation within the current authored selector. Across all five, the production footprint P and fixed sleeve K have Jaccard overlap `.919–.954`; they retain the same branched object identity at this raster scale. H, the existing convex lamina countermodel, fills the gaps by definition and spans `1.93–2.46×` P's source-cell area. These relations are similar to frozen P1/P2 references (`P/K=.961/.936`; `H/P=2.28/2.24`). More varied natural scopes did not reverse the existing visual distinction or make a BODY winner evident.

U/W/S are not shown as reconstructed candidates for these later events. Their exact definitions require a commit-time chemical B raster, which the frozen later-commit inputs do not contain. Atlas B marks all three omitted for every selected row; no nearest-time map, scalar mean, or replay approximation was substituted.

**No BODY candidate is selected.** Structural diversity makes the existing sleeve-versus-lamina distinction easier to inspect on larger and more branched scopes. It does not show that a visually larger difference is better supported or artistically preferable.

- **P:** actual frozen production descriptor mask.
- **K:** union of fixed `.012` node sections around actual I.
- **H:** convex lattice envelope of the exact K cells. This is the existing cohesive-lamina countermodel, not measured support or inferred cohesion.
- **U/W/S:** unchanged existing B-based definitions, omitted for later commits because the required exact B maps are unavailable.

Atlas A uses the same 96×96 coordinate frame and 320×320 nearest-neighbor display for every panel. The dark line/dot overlay marks actual reconstructed I nodes/edges; it is context, not extra body geometry. Atlas B repeats K and shows explicit missing-data cards for U/W/S.

**P versus K.** P/K Jaccard values are `.925, .925, .954, .919, .932` for P4/P22/P25/P23/P3. Their relation is close to the two early references. P's production width adaptation adds/removes cells but does not visibly create a different body object in this fixed-scale atlas. Jaccard is descriptive, not an identity threshold.

**K connectivity and legibility.** P, K, and H are each one four-connected source mask in all five selected rows. K keeps the complete branched I overlay visible without a special junction rule. This is source-grid geometry only; compiler, solver, renderer, audio, and perceptual behavior were not tested.

**K versus H.** H adds `1,105–3,280` cells over K in the selected examples. Its outlines remain convex and span the concavities between branches, including P3's compact scope and P25's high-junction scope. This is the declared H construction. The atlas cannot turn its new sheet area into environmental support or an artistic preference.

**U/W/S.** No later-case local-chemical claim is supported. Whether W remains a small U modulation, whether S becomes disconnected, or whether any such behavior follows scope structure cannot be assessed without the exact commit-time B arrays. The earlier P1/P2 atlas remains available as its own reference and is unchanged.

## Interpretation and stop

**Measured observation:** current natural scopes include much larger and more branched graphs than P1/P2 while keeping source-annulus-to-boundary organization. Under those scopes, P/K remain close and H remains a spanning sheet.

**Research interpretation:** the extra cases confirm that visible P/K/H contrasts persist across structural extremes in this one deterministic sequence. They do not establish a preferred body ontology, a material law, a distribution across seeds, or an unconstrained natural morphology.

**Artistic implication:** artist can compare a structure-following sleeve with a cohesive sheet on larger branching examples. No winner is issued here.

No synthetic appendix was needed: the frozen scopes already vary across the measured structural axes. Stop at the visual comparison package. No production choice, U/W/S approximation, audio check, synthetic graph, new simulation, or next technical pass is proposed.
