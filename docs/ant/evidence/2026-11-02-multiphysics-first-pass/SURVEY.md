# ANT multiphysics first-pass survey

- **Date:** 2026-11-02
- **Reviewed code/evidence:** worktree `796d5c7` (wet-film probe branch)
- **Purpose:** broad physical candidate survey, not a final architecture or roadmap decision.

## Result

Several proposals have real physical precedents, but each precedent depends on state variables, geometry, forcing, and constitutive laws that ANT does not currently carry. A phenomenon existing in granular, fluid, elastic, or acoustic systems does not establish that the current ANT input produces it. No candidate below is a verified ANT mechanism.

The completed wet-film campaign is a useful, narrow exception: its isolated numerical model transports a Newtonian precursor film under a declared capillary law, with mass and energy checks. It does **not** model fragments, viscoelastic curing, a stress-bearing solid, or an acoustic body. Its conditional ANT arm uses assumed water dose and one-second contact bins; it is not observed water or measured foot traction. I read the existing 25-run report and did not rerun it. [Wet-film report](../2026-11-02-wet-film-formation-first-probe/REPORT.md).

Current production has a different and much narrower material chain. `SurfaceField` stores dimensionless 0–1 compaction and reads contact positions; `TrailPlateBirth` combines a normalized chemical memory with `SurfaceField.sampleSupport()` and authored corridor gates to create immutable descriptors; `MaterialStratigraphy` appends those masks into per-cell columns and counts foot positions for later qualification. These are implemented mechanisms, not an advecting liquid, grain contact network, stress tensor, measured traction, or moving 3D body. The reviewed `FOOT_CONTACT` use in these modules supplies no calibrated normal force, shear traction, impulse, pressure, or water quantity. See [SurfaceField](../../../../src/lib/agents/ant/SurfaceField.js), [TrailPlateBirth](../../../../src/lib/agents/ant/TrailPlateBirth.js), [MaterialStratigraphy](../../../../src/lib/agents/ant/MaterialStratigraphy.js), [current ANT guide](../../README.md), and the [world-model design contract](../../world-model.md).

The survey treats the three pasted Argon rounds as archived hypotheses only, as their own README requires. Their proposed scenes, costs, and sounds are not scientific evidence. Primary literature below supports only the bounded phenomena described beside each citation.

## The first seven candidates

### 1. Granular jamming and force-chain scattering

**Physical footing.** Frictional grains can form load-bearing jammed states, and shear can produce anisotropic, fragile force networks. In 3D experiments, contact forces and heterogeneity were measured in confined quartz-grain packs. Those findings support force-chain mechanics in specified particulate assemblies, not a general rule that repeated paths create a grain skeleton. [Bi et al., “Jamming by shear,” *Nature* (2011)](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature10667); [Hurley et al., “Quantifying Interparticle Forces and Heterogeneity in 3D Granular Materials,” *Physical Review Letters* (2016)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.117.098005).

**ANT gap.** A scalar compaction memory does not reveal particle positions, packing fraction, contacts, normal forces, tangential forces, confinement, or frictional history. A force-chain graph is a consequence of those contact mechanics, not a synonym for a path skeleton. Hertzian contact stiffness alone does not imply a jammed network, arch growth, or a crackling event distribution; the latter depends on loading, boundaries, contact failure, and stress redistribution.

**Smallest discriminating test, not run here.** Use a small, explicit 2D or 3D grain pack with declared radii, contact/friction law, confinement, and quasi-static load. Compare below- and above-jamming or shear-jammed states using both a mechanical rigidity measure and a small probe-pulse transmission measure. Preserve the same grain inventory and boundary controls. Do not infer jamming from a high contact count alone.

### 2. Eroded voids and acoustic cavities

**Physical footing.** A cavity and an opening can form a coupled acoustic resonator; the familiar lumped Helmholtz approximation depends on cavity volume, neck area, and effective neck length. Leaks materially change measured transmission loss, and an open system can still have leaky resonances rather than switching between “no resonance” and “resonance.” [Selamet, Kim & Huff, leakage experiments in Helmholtz resonators, *JASA* (2009), author-hosted paper](https://mae.osu.edu/sites/default/files/2021-12/J78.pdf); [Ma et al., “Sound trapping in an open resonator,” *Nature Communications* (2021)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-021-25130-4).

**ANT gap.** A 2D ring, mask hole, or chronological overlap does not enclose a 3D air volume. The proposal needs actual removal/transport of solid material, a roof or enclosing surface, connected air volume, aperture geometry, leakage, and a wall excitation path. A cavity model also needs its own gas state or a justified lumped approximation. The current wet-film obstacle is an excluded 2D domain region, not an excavated, deforming, or acoustically enclosed volume.

**Smallest discriminating test, not run here.** Hold the solid-wall geometry and excitation fixed while comparing a genuinely enclosed cavity with a measured neck, a deliberately leaky version, and an open groove. Measure resonance frequency and Q/transmission. A rendered loop alone is not a closure test. Any separate project cavity geometry screen is not counted as solid–air coupling unless pressure and wall motion exchange work.

### 3. Tectonic stress accumulation and rupture

**Physical footing.** Laboratory fault experiments produce stick-slip or dynamic rupture under controlled normal and shear loading, roughness, friction, and apparatus stiffness. Measurements find nontrivial friction evolution with slip and slip rate; a threshold cellular rule does not inherit earthquake-like statistics merely because it avalanches. [Rubino, Rosakis & Lapusta, “Understanding dynamic friction through spontaneously evolving laboratory earthquakes,” *Nature Communications* (2017)](https://doi.org/10.1038/ncomms15991).

**ANT gap.** Contact frequency is not strain energy or stress. A rupture model needs a mechanically loaded body or fault, elastic stress transfer, prestress/loading boundary conditions, a slip or damage law, and energy release. A “critical” compaction threshold alone cannot support claims about faults, aftershocks, or Gutenberg–Richter scaling. Current ANT has no such stress-loading chain.

**Smallest discriminating test, not run here.** Build a one-dimensional spring–slider or short fault analogue with explicit drive stiffness, normal load, friction/state law, and energy ledger. Compare a stable-sliding parameter regime against a rate-weakening regime under the same drive. First establish the expected stick-slip boundary; only then ask whether a spatially extended fault gives a nontrivial rupture-size distribution.

### 4. Phononic terrain and a single heterogeneous elastic medium

**Physical footing.** Periodic elastic/acoustic impedance contrasts can create direction- and frequency-dependent band gaps. In one experimental phononic slab, the measured gap depended on propagation along the periodic direction; transverse behavior was different. Bragg scattering needs spatial scales related to the wave wavelength and sufficient contrast. Disorder-induced Anderson localization is a separate mechanism and should not be conflated with a periodic band gap. [Hsiao et al., “Waveguiding inside the complete band gap of a phononic crystal slab,” *Physical Review E* (2007)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.76.056601); [“One-Dimensional Hypersonic Phononic Crystals,” *Nano Letters* (2010)](https://doi.org/10.1021/nl903959r).

**ANT gap.** A material mask or irregular route network does not by itself provide a wavelength-scale periodic medium, a calibrated density/modulus field, or a solved elastic wave equation. A single evolving continuum is a coherent representation if the intended material remains one continuous medium; it suppresses independent layer motion and cannot by itself represent advected liquid or sealed air. This is a modeling choice, not a free performance result.

**Smallest discriminating test, not run here.** Compare finite-strip transmission through a periodic impedance pattern and a randomized pattern with the same length and mean material properties, over a frequency sweep. Vary lattice spacing relative to wavelength. A gap that survives only for a selected spacing/propagation direction is a specific phononic result, not a universal terrain effect.

### 5. Viscoelastic coagulation and a surface-tension web

**Physical footing.** Liquid drops can coalesce through a growing neck, with the dynamics changing across inertial and viscous regimes and depending on viscosity, density, surface tension, and scale. Wet granular packs can also acquire strong capillary cohesion and change structure under tapping. These support liquid coalescence and wet-grain effects under specified materials; they do not establish that ant trails become viscoelastic filaments, fuse, shrink, or cure into elastic solids. [“Coalescence of bubbles and drops in an outer fluid,” *Nature Communications* (2014)](https://doi.org/10.1038/ncomms4182); [“Similarity of wet granular packing to gels,” *Nature Communications* (2014)](https://doi.org/10.1038/ncomms6014).

**ANT gap.** The current wet-film result is a positive-height Newtonian precursor-film calculation with an assumed wetting potential and fluid reservoir. It has no dry front, viscoelastic stress, adhesion, curing, fracture, or rigid fiber identity. Two initially crossed ridges in that campaign are already connected; they do not test independent fibers fusing. A “Steiner” or 120° web is not an automatic consequence of surface tension for a solidifying line network. Plateau rules concern equilibrium interfaces/films in particular geometries and constraints, not arbitrary ANT path intersections.

**Smallest discriminating test, not run here.** Compare two initially separate materialized filaments with declared volume, surface tension, viscosity/relaxation time, substrate wetting, and endpoint constraints. Track neck growth and mass; then add a separate cure law only in a later arm and test whether a load-bearing solid remains after the liquid moves. Keep the fluid-only control as a control, not as evidence of solid formation.

### 6. Chrono-scattering and graph waveguides

**Physical footing.** Spatial waveguides and scattering junctions are valid reductions when their propagation speeds, impedances, lengths, and boundary conditions represent an actual structure. Time-modulated acoustic media can generate frequency conversion and mode changes, but experiments use deliberately controlled modulation; the time-varying medium itself can do work on the wave. [Zhang et al., “Towards altering sound frequency at will by a linear meta-layer with time-varying and quantized properties,” *Communications Physics* (2021)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42005-021-00721-1).

**ANT gap.** Repeated visitation between points is not a physical delay line unless edge delay is `length / wave speed` and junction scattering satisfies a declared force/flow (or pressure/volume-flow) law. Letting ant-count set impedance, or defining nodes from stops/turns, is an authored map. ANT material evolution occurs over seconds to minutes while an acoustic field cycles in milliseconds or faster. Slow changes can shift a mode quasi-statically; calling them “temporal scattering” needs evidence of dynamic sidebands or mode conversion, not just a changed pitch after the structure changes.

**Smallest discriminating test, not run here.** First compare a fixed 1D graph waveguide with an equivalent reference rod/line at the same geometry and impedance. Separately modulate one edge at a declared fraction of the acoustic frequency and compare slow versus fast modulation; measure sideband energy and account for energy supplied by the modulation. This distinguishes static graph acoustics from true time modulation.

### 7. Reciprocal cymatics

**Physical footing.** Chladni patterns arise from a driven plate’s resonant modes and mobile particles collecting around low-amplitude nodal regions. Patterns vary with plate geometry, boundary support, excitation frequency, and drive location. This is a real drive–structure–particle interaction, but “cymatics” is not a material law by itself. [“Exploration of Resonant Modes for Circular and Polygonal Chladni Plates” (2024)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10969725/).

**ANT gap.** Reciprocity would require vibration to move mobile material, and that redistribution to change mass/stiffness/contact topology enough to alter later vibration. A one-way oscillator-to-visualizer relation does not supply that loop. Current ANT contact points are not calibrated acoustic forces, and the project design contract does not authorize master-output audio feedback into world state. A future local mechanical path could be assessed as its own bounded law; it must not smuggle the audio bus or a heading command into behavior.

**Smallest discriminating test, not run here.** Drive one plate with a measured mode shape and frequency; compare a mobile-particle layer against the same layer immobilized and against no particles. Measure particle redistribution, mass/topology change, and the post-redistribution modal shift. Track input and dissipated energy. This tests both directions of the purported reciprocity.

## Round 2: representation options and integrated proposals

These are not interchangeable physics models:

- **Micro-bonds:** a bond is a constitutive relation, not simply a stored edge. It must specify whether it carries tension/compression, shear, bending/rotation, viscous loss, failure, healing, and rest-length change. One `k_ij` cannot stand in for capillary transport, frictional contact, curing, and acoustic pressure without erasing which quantity is conserved and what can move.
- **Sparse mass–spring network:** useful for a chosen discrete structure, but connectivity does not guarantee rigidity and central-force springs do not automatically model a plate’s bending. A network needs enough independent constraints or explicit bending/angle terms. Beroz et al. model disordered fibre-network mechanics and local stiffness sensing; that modeled heterogeneity is not an experiment establishing every stretch/bend/floppy regime, nor a validation of an ANT spring network. Sparse does not mean mechanically complete. [Beroz et al., “Physical limits to biomechanical sensing in disordered fibre networks,” *Nature Communications* (2017)](https://doi.org/10.1038/ncomms16096).
- **Beam vs shell vs volume mesh:** a beam carries line geometry and bending/torsion; a shell carries surface geometry and membrane/bending response; a volume mesh resolves 3D occupancy/strain. These models need different state and boundaries. A 3D visualization is not proof of 3D support, volume, or stress. Joining time-separated footprints with a loft can add material that was never deposited.
- **3D material graph:** adjacency is provenance/topology. It is not automatically a mechanical connection. Contact, bond, overlap, crossing, and support need distinct predicates and geometric maps. Intersections need a declared rule for whether layers touch, slide, fuse, or remain separated.
- **Modal/reduced acoustic model:** reduction may be valuable after geometry and mechanics are defined. Modes depend on mass, stiffness, damping, supports, and acoustic boundaries; changing topology requires updating or transferring that model and validating the residual error/state continuity. A modal basis is an acoustic representation, not a formation process or a universal runtime guarantee.

The three integrated sketches remain coherent only under separate conditions:

| Sketch | What it can represent | Missing physical commitment / decisive check |
|---|---|---|
| **I. Anisotropic fiber-sintered substrate** | One continuous medium with evolving direction-dependent stiffness or wave speed. | Define what state evolves the anisotropy and whether material is actually deposited/sintered. It does not represent free liquid transport or independent layers without extra state. Compare measured directional wave propagation before and after one controlled formation input. |
| **II. Sintered skeleton plus acoustic pockets** | Solid load paths and air volumes can coexist. This is the most direct solid–air interaction target. | A planar loop is not a sealed cavity. Require a 3D volume/neck/leak map and pressure-to-wall force / wall-normal velocity transfer. Compare open, leaky, and enclosed configurations. |
| **III. Slow morphodynamics plus reduced modes** | Separates slow geometry change from fast vibration; this is a plausible computational organization. | It is not one material law. Validate the geometry-to-mass/stiffness/modal map, energy and state transfer at revisions, and held-out responses against a higher-fidelity reference. No runtime gain follows from the sketch alone. |

## Round 3 additions

- **A. Wet damper → dry resonator:** a moisture/cure-dependent structure can change both storage and loss response. But “wet means muted; dry means long ring” is not universal. Wood experiments show material-, moisture-state-, direction-, and adsorption/desorption-dependent damping; one study found a change in oak damping across bound-water conditions but not capillary water. Treat water content, mass, modulus, and loss as separate variables, and do not infer a monotone law from age. [Lu et al., moisture sorption and wood vibration, *Forest Products Journal* (2012)](https://doi.org/10.13073/0015-7473-62.3.171); [Zieliński, oak moisture and free-vibration damping, *Archives of Acoustics*](https://acoustics.ippt.pan.pl/index.php/aa/article/view/416). Small test: a fixed specimen/material, measured moisture adsorption and desorption, then ring-down or frequency response at matched support and excitation. This tests wet × damping; cure × elastic is a separate measurement.
- **B. Orientation defects / grain boundaries:** nematic order and topological defects are legitimate state in liquid-crystal/active-nematic systems. A high gradient of a direction field is not by itself a topological defect: a defect needs nonzero winding around a loop and loss/variation of local order at its core. ANT ant travel direction is a behavior-derived field, not a proven crystal director; polar current and nematic orientation are different summaries. [Dell’Arciprete et al., active-nematic colony and Q-tensor defects, *Nature Communications* (2018)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-018-06370-3). Small read-only screen: compute polar and nematic order separately from saved movement segments, then measure winding and order magnitude on closed loops; compare defect cores to route crossings and current Plate masks. This can test whether a distinct orientational state exists in observed trajectories, but not whether it causes deposition. The assigned orientation screen is not counted as completed evidence here.
- **C. Mobile fragments → pinned reef:** mobile rigid or deformable fragments can translate/rotate, collide, and become trapped or bonded. Those transitions require actual force/torque, mass/inertia, geometry, friction, and separate contact versus permanent-bond rules. Current `FOOT_CONTACT` position/count does not supply horizontal traction impulse. Small test: one mobile fragment and one fixed obstacle under a declared tangential impulse, then a two-fragment collision control; check linear/angular momentum, sliding, escape, snagging, and any explicitly defined pin/bond state. The assigned fragment screen is not counted as completed coupled evidence here.

## Timescales and the smallest shared interface

The world model explicitly allows processes on different timescales. That is compatible with multiple physics; it does not mean all models should be coupled at one rate. Current production already separates contact/movement updates, one-second `TrailPlateBirth` analysis, 20-second chemical-memory/persistence gates, the 1,800-second `SurfaceField` half-life, and faster audio integration (the prior audio report describes its 6 kHz internal grid). Slow material evolution could therefore update geometry/material coefficients less often than acoustic state. But a 60 Hz geometry commit is not automatically smooth or energy-neutral, especially if topology changes abruptly; low-frequency motion may not be adiabatic just because the solver is fast.

The minimum useful common contract is data and causality, not a new multiphysics framework:

1. **Typed state:** name the quantity and physical unit (or an explicitly fixed dimensionless scale), time, and spatial support. Examples: liquid height `m`; pressure/stress `Pa`; displacement `m`; density `kg/m³`; orientation tensor dimensionless; contact force `N` or impulse `N·s`. Do not reinterpret normalized compaction or foot counts as these quantities.
2. **Geometric mapping:** declare whether a value belongs to a cell volume, face, point, segment, shell, or contact. State the map between the ANT normalized domain and physical lengths. Field remapping between grids should preserve the relevant integral (mass, force, or work) instead of copying a color/scalar by index.
3. **Transfer law and budget:** specify which quantity crosses each interface and where it goes: mass flux for liquid; equal/opposite contact force and torque for fragments; pressure traction and normal velocity/work for air–solid; force and displacement/work for mechanical support. Account for sources, dissipation, radiation, and numerical residuals in the appropriate budget.
4. **Causality and clocks:** state what reads the previous completed tick versus current state, how slower sources are held/interpolated, and whether a link is one-way or reciprocal. Sound is an output, not a world-state source; any vibration→rearrangement link must be a local mechanical force/energy path with a declared bound.

This contract lets one narrow causal link be tested at a time. It does not require a universal material network, a universal `k` field, or a single solver. Preserve formation provenance separately from current mobile state; do not use age alone as depth or use an attractive image/audio result as a physical law.

## Counterargument to the previous sparse stratified network

The sparse stratified network remains a serious **acoustic representation** option. The prior audio diagnosis measured a shared worklet with distinct masked layers, inter-layer links, direct exposure routing, and retained vibration state. The measured local-X follow-up also found many tiny compiled layers and identical isolated one-node responses, while explicitly warning that isolated equality did not prove equality of fully coupled state or audible contribution. Those findings argue against equating Plate count with acoustic complexity; they do not show that every layer is redundant or that a consolidated field is more physical. See the report and source-only diagnosis at [commit `918764c`](https://github.com/oseconds/ant-2026/tree/918764c2d4924eb89fd6874122edc30b73d1499c/docs/ant/evidence/2026-11-01-local-x-audio-reassessment).

Strongest reasons to retain that architecture as a reference: it preserves chronological layers and local coupling; it can represent buried material receiving indirect energy; and a sparse, validated acoustic model can be useful even when slow formation is richer. Strongest reasons not to treat it as the universal material model: a layer per deposit can bake administrative identity into solver state; coarse occupancy and pickup placement can impose grid artifacts; and a spring/link representation cannot stand in for liquid transport, granular contacts, curing, rupture, or air pressure without their separate state and constitutive laws. Keep the network as one downstream mechanics/acoustics hypothesis and compare it against a physical process only after stating what state/transfer that process requires.

## Literature limits and review boundary

The linked primary studies demonstrate phenomena in their own test geometries and materials. They do not calibrate ANT dimensions, water, friction, elastic moduli, contact forces, time constants, audience perception, browser cost, or artistic acceptance. The candidate micro-experiments above are proposals, not executed tests. No new simulation or source code was run or changed for this survey; no candidate is ranked as a final architecture, and no production coupling is recommended by this document.
