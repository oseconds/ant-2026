# Survey source audit

- **Scope:** Primary-source metadata and adjacent claim fit in `SURVEY.md`; literature validation only. No experiments run and no adoption decision implied.
- **Outcome:** One author-attribution error and one citation-scope overclaim. Other sampled claims are appropriately bounded to their source systems, and the survey states the ANT transfer gaps.

## Correction

At `SURVEY.md:96`, the citation for DOI [10.1038/s41467-018-06370-3](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-018-06370-3) is misattributed as “Doostmohammadi et al.” The publisher identifies the paper as **Dell’Arciprete et al., “A growing bacterial colony in two dimensions as an active nematic,” Nature Communications 9, 4190 (2018)**. It reports Q-tensor/topological-defect analysis in growing *E. coli* colonies. “Doostmohammadi et al.” is associated with a different 2018 review, DOI 10.1038/s41467-018-05666-8. Correct the author attribution while retaining the cited DOI/title context. Primary source: [Nature Communications article](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-018-06370-3).

At `SURVEY.md:80`, [Beroz et al. (2017)](https://www.nature.com/articles/ncomms16096) is a model-based mechanosensing study using disordered fiber networks, including architectures derived from imaged collagen/fibrin. Its abstract supports broad local-stiffness heterogeneity and sensing limits. The wording “Experimental fiber networks show distinct stretch-dominated, bend-dominated, and floppy regimes as connectivity changes” overstates what this cited work itself establishes: those network regimes are model/rigidity concepts, not a direct experimental result reported by this citation. Rephrase as a modeled network result or cite a primary experiment specifically establishing those regimes.

## Verified citations and claim fit

Publisher or primary full-text records checked for these cited works; titles, DOI targets, author/year information (where given), and the nearby bounded claim fit were consistent:

- Granular jamming / force chains: Bi et al. (2011), [10.1038/nature10667](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature10667); Hurley et al. (2016), [10.1103/PhysRevLett.117.098005](https://journals.aps.org/prl/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevLett.117.098005). The cited findings are tied to shear-jammed/frictional granular assemblies and compressed quartz-grain experiments.
- Leaky acoustic resonators: Ma et al. (2021), [10.1038/s41467-021-25130-4](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-021-25130-4), supports leaky modes in an open acoustic resonator. Selamet, Kim & Huff, [10.1121/1.3183416](https://doi.org/10.1121/1.3183416), DOI resolves as linked but metadata/abstract could not be independently retrieved in this audit; **metadata unverified here**. The survey’s general caution about leaky resonances is supported by Ma et al.
- Laboratory rupture: Rubino, Rosakis & Lapusta (2017), [10.1038/ncomms15991](https://www.nature.com/articles/ncomms15991); experimental dynamic friction and rupture conditions fit the adjacent claim.
- Phononic band gaps: Hsiao et al. (2007), [10.1103/PhysRevE.76.056601](https://journals.aps.org/pre/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevE.76.056601); Gomopoulos et al. (2010), [10.1021/nl903959r](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/nl903959r). The cited direction/frequency dependence is supported in the source geometries.
- Liquid coalescence and wet granular packing: Paulsen et al. (2014), [10.1038/ncomms4182](https://www.nature.com/articles/ncomms4182); Li et al. (2014), [10.1038/ncomms6014](https://www.nature.com/articles/ncomms6014). The survey correctly limits coalescence to liquid neck growth and wet-grain findings to their tested packing conditions.
- Time-modulated acoustics: Zhang et al. (2021), [10.1038/s42005-021-00721-1](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42005-021-00721-1). Measured sidebands/frequency conversion under deliberate modulation support the distinction from slow structural change.
- Chladni modes: Val Baker et al. (2024), [10.3390/e26030264](https://www.mdpi.com/1099-4300/26/3/264). Title, author list/year, and resonant plate/nodal-pattern scope match.
- Disordered fiber networks: Beroz et al. (2017), [10.1038/ncomms16096](https://www.nature.com/articles/ncomms16096). The source supports heterogeneous local stiffness and sensing limits in modeled networks; see the wording correction above on network regimes/connectivity.
- Moisture and curing: Lu et al. (2012), [10.13073/0015-7473-62.3.171](https://doi.org/10.13073/0015-7473-62.3.171); Zieliński, [Archives of Acoustics article](https://acoustics.ippt.pan.pl/index.php/aa/article/view/416); Hamdan et al. (1998), [10.1088/0954-0083/10/4/001](https://doi.org/10.1088/0954-0083/10/4/001). Lu concerns moisture sorption state and wood vibration; Zieliński specifically reports damping changes across inhibition-water but not capillary-water range and adsorption/desorption differences; Hamdan reports storage modulus/loss tangent changes during SMC cure. This fits the survey’s explicit rejection of a universal monotone wet/dry rule.
- Anisotropic imbibition: Maier et al., [10.1177/00405175241268794](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/00405175241268794), first published online 2024. It studies capillary imbibition in thin anisotropic fibrous media (PET yarns with PEG/EG), supporting the narrow orientation-linked wicking precedent, not ANT’s behavior-derived direction field.
- Solid–air FSI: Dhulipala et al. (2022), [10.1002/eqe.3659](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/eqe.3659); Curti & Cura (2001), [10.2495/FSI010251](https://www.witpress.com/elibrary/wit-transactions-on-the-built-environment/56/3580). These concern acoustic FSI capability validation and a flexible-panel cavity experiment, respectively; neither is represented as ANT verification.
- Vibration-driven settlement: Brumund & Leonards (1972), [10.1061/JSFEAQ.0001731](https://ascelibrary.org/doi/10.1061/JSFEAQ.0001731). Its sand tests directly support the caution that settlement depends on transmitted energy and is not generally correlated with acceleration alone.
