# Finite peak–saddle domain reproducibility

## Decision

**DOMAIN FAMILY PARTIALLY REPRODUCIBLE**

Under the primary 8-neighbor construction, many finite domains keep direct spatial overlap across adjacent saved states. Both late 1 s winner switches are explained by contrast rank crossing between two separately available spatial domains, not by disappearance of the former winner's domain. The old and new winners are disjoint from each other at the target state; each has its own overlapping predecessor. But the family is not representation invariant: the predeclared 4-neighbor convention changes finite/essential status, domain census and the global winner substantially; both conventions also produce many-to-many overlap graphs. Keep finite-domain availability as a research-only EMERGENCE primitive. Do not promote a proposer or resolve ranking here.

**[Architecture inference]** The previous zero-overlap winner-spine result does not imply that its finite peak–saddle domain disappeared. At both observed late switches, the old finite domain remains spatially present and the new winner already has a strong predecessor-domain overlap. This isolates global contrast ranking as the immediate switch mechanism for these two 8-neighbor cases. It does not establish a stable identity law: correspondence here means cell overlap only.

**[Boundary]** B is an existing chemical/history readout. Its finite lobes are mathematical organizations of B restricted to E, not natural material objects. This result does not validate material meaning, a selector, UPDATE, accumulation or BODY.

## Implementation fact and parent representation

Current `TrailPlateBirth` updates a 96×96 chemical-memory array with a 20 s exponential memory constant and bounded pheromone input. Its production qualification combines chemical threshold `0.30` with sampled SurfaceField support `0.015` at radius `0.025`; production selection then uses the existing source annulus, observation-boundary eligibility and route projection. This pass does not call or replace that production selector.

The parent B countermodel instead computes a 0-D superlevel merge structure from saved `B_memory` restricted to saved binary `E_qualified`. Equal values activate atomically. A connected maximum plateau is one peak. Elder preference is higher birth level, then lower row-major peak index. A finite lobe is the strict-superlevel E component `B > deathLevel`; the parent winner is greatest finite contrast, ties by lower peak index. Essential peaks have no internal death saddle and are retained in this census, not converted to finite candidates.

The new measurement enumerates all peaks and finite lobes under 8-neighbor adjacency and the declared 4-neighbor corner-contact sensitivity. No lobe is filtered by size, contrast, appearance or rank. Temporal and same-state adjacency correspondence stores every nonzero-overlap pair, with intersection, Jaccard and reciprocal-best receipts; zero-overlap items are listed. Best-by-intersection ties use greater Jaccard then lower counterpart peak index; best-by-Jaccard ties use greater intersection then lower counterpart index. Neither is a domain identity threshold. Split and merge counts mean source/target degree greater than one in this overlap graph, not a claim about physical genealogy.

### Winner-switch decomposition

Both adjacent 1 s winner changes are ranking crossovers among spatially present finite domains:

- **P1, 82.016667 → 83.016667 s:** peak 3799 remains the exact 4-cell domain (`4/4`, Jaccard `1.000`) but falls from contrast `.288266` to `.278969`, rank 2. Peak 4455 was already a 42-cell finite lobe at the earlier state (contrast `.274679`); it becomes 43 cells with contrast `.280049` and Jaccard `42/43 = .976744`. Both domains persist while their contrast order reverses.
- **P2, 104.016667 → 105.016667 s:** peak 4264 retains `17/18` cells (Jaccard `.944444`) while its contrast falls `.339831 → .319226`, rank 2. Peak 4259 already exists as the same 13-cell lobe at both states (Jaccard `1.000`); its contrast rises `.318001 → .323514` and it becomes winner. Again, both domains are available and ranking crosses.

The 1 s follow-up transitions keep the new winner unchanged with exact domain Jaccard 1: P1 peak 4455 (`43/43`) and P2 peak 4259 (`13/13`). The prior B spine switches therefore reflect a downstream change in which finite domain wins and which spine is projected, not the disappearance of the old finite domain at these anchors.

The previous and new winners themselves are disjoint at each switch target (`oldWinnerToNewWinnerOverlap: null`). “Ranking crossover” here means each of two distinct domains has a separate spatial predecessor and their contrast ranks exchange; it does not mean one lobe morphs into the other.

Across all eight saved-state transitions under 8-neighbor adjacency, four winners change; each prior winner has at least one nonzero-overlap successor and the deterministic best-overlap counterpart no longer ranks first. The 10 s and 8 s gaps include greater spatial deformation and should not be read as adjacent stability. The full source/target counterpart rankings, winner contrasts and every pair receipt are in `temporalTransitions`.

### 4-neighbor temporal sensitivity

The 4-neighbor census also has spatially overlapping domains at every 1 s step, but with still larger many-to-many overlap counts. Its late winners are stable within that convention: P1 peak 642 persists with Jaccard `.9881` then `.9651`; P2 peak 4093 persists with `.8947` then `1.000`. These are different global winners from 8-neighbor adjacency, so their internal stability does not resolve which convention should define a domain.

## Same-state 8-vs-4 sensitivity and essential peaks

Across the ten same-state comparisons:

- 8-neighbor finite counts are `105–203`, with `31–44` essential peaks; 4-neighbor finite counts are `125–274`, with `86–124` essentials.
- The finite-domain correspondence census has 22–33 8-neighbor lobes and 16–41 4-neighbor lobes without any nonzero-overlap counterpart. There are 52–117 one-to-many 8→4 sources and 55–132 4-neighbor targets with multiple 8-neighbor predecessors.
- Every 8-neighbor peak plateau overlaps at least one 4-neighbor peak plateau. In contrast, 70–155 4-neighbor peak plateaus per state have no overlapping 8-neighbor peak plateau.
- There are 28–39 overlapping peak-plateau pairs per state that change status from finite under 8-neighbor to essential under 4-neighbor; no reverse status flip appears in these same-state receipts.
- Greatest-contrast winner domains overlap between the two conventions in only P1-S2; the P1 and P2 COMMIT winners are spatially disjoint across the conventions.

This is a substantial dependence on corner-contact representation, not a reason to choose whichever winner is more stable. The 4-neighbor rule remains sensitivity-only.

The essential-root question remains open in one specific way. The strongest essential peak in each late 1 s window is stable at its peak cell and has no internal death saddle: P1 peak 6576 supports an E component of 869 → 889 → 901 cells from COMMIT−2 through COMMIT; P2 peak 6478 supports 1,163 → 1,166 → 1,172 cells. These broad essential components coexist with many small finite lobes. Excluding a persistent essential root is therefore consequential to the *candidate family definition*, but this pass did not turn it into a finite candidate or run a counterfactual selector. The measurements cannot attribute the finite winner switches causally to essential exclusion.

## Interpretation, counterexamples and limits

**[Measurement result]** In two late 1 s changes, the old and new 8-neighbor winners coexist as high-overlap spatial domains and their contrast ordering reverses. Many other domains also have direct overlaps; some have no overlap, and extensive split/merge degrees prevent a simple persistent-object account.

**[Representation sensitivity]** 4/8 adjacency changes counts, essentiality and winner locations. At both commits, the global winners are disjoint between conventions. No universal cell-scale unit is established.

**[Essential-root counterexample]** Large, same-cell essential peaks persist at the late anchors but do not have an internal finite saddle. Their broad E-component support could matter to a later interpretation, yet no alternative essential-root law was tested.

**[Research interpretation]** Finite-domain availability merits narrowly bounded further study independently from global contrast ranking. The domain family remains research-only because the tessellation and finite/essential partition depend strongly on the predeclared contact convention and correspondence is often many-to-many.

**[Artistic/modeling implication]** No material meaning or visual preference was judged. A chemical lobe is not thereby a Plate, and this evidence does not support size, BODY, thickness, stiffness, event identity, UPDATE, hysteresis or production mapping.

Limits: one seed and two events; only ten sparse snapshots, with 10 s and 8 s early gaps; one 96×96 representation; no replay, later fields, independent run or other adjacency; E itself changes; overlap does not establish object identity or causal continuity. No threshold, smoothing or post-hoc lobe selection was used.
