# 8-neighbor finite-domain ensemble interpretation

## Decision

**`DOMAIN AVAILABILITY EXISTS BUT IS TOO UNDERDETERMINED`**

The 8-neighbor B construction yields finite spatial domains, including larger and multi-anchor examples. It does not yield a manageable, intrinsically distinguished materialization set. The ensemble remains dense: 1,665 finite-domain observations across ten anchors, median size 2 cells, 61.7% at size 1–2, and no size or contrast separation that isolates a distinct persistent class. Many correspondence families last across saved anchors, but many contain micro-lobes and participate in split/merge overlap.

**Architecture implication:** finite mathematical spatial opportunities exist before UPDATE. A rule for choosing one does not. Keep peak–saddle availability research-only; stop before capture, ranking, UPDATE or BODY.

## Interpretation and next step

**Measurement:** the ensemble is not only one-frame micro-noise. Many domain correspondences extend across saved anchors, and a larger persistent tail exists. At the same time, 61.7% of all finite-domain observations are only 1–2 cells; even five-anchor families have median size 2. Many domains nest, most tracks remain small, and repeated split/merge is common.

**Research interpretation:** finite spatial availability is real under the authored 8-neighbor representation, but it is too numerous and underdetermined to serve as a materialization candidate set by itself. The evidence does not establish a natural scale, material meaning or rule for capturing one domain.

**Artistic/modeling implication:** none adopted. No proposer, ranking, capture rule, event ID, UPDATE/hysteresis, BODY, material mapping or production state was created.

**Smallest justified next step:** stop the peak–saddle family at this boundary. The current evidence does not justify another capture-rule pass. Reopen only with a separately authored question that supplies meaning without hiding the present multiplicity behind a threshold.

## Limits

One seed, two actual event sequences, five saved snapshots per event and uneven 10/8/1/1-second gaps. E changes between snapshots; no continuous lifetime is known. The frozen 96×96 fields are one spatial resolution. F/G/H/I are existing derived structural representations and share provenance with E. Spatial correspondence does not establish object identity. No material or artistic value was tested.
