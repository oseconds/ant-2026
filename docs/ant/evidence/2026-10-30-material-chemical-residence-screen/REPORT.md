# Material changes chemical residence: independent world screen

## Decision

**PROMISING as a distinct research responsibility, with mixed world consequences; not ready for adoption.** Material-conditioned residence changes an existing shared field's future loss, including during source-free intervals. The frozen-source probe established that temporal distinction. This independent coupled screen shows that it reaches subsequent movement, qualification and immutable material history without a new source law, destination command or allocation scheduler.

This verdict does not mean more chemical, broader qualification or deeper layering is desirable. The initially provisional MIXED assessment described those world consequences. Integrated assessment retains those mixed consequences but identifies a distinct responsibility worth one further discriminating research pass. There is no evidence yet that **local placement of material** adds value beyond a bulk increase in chemical retention, nor that ANT should give its material this meaning.

The coordinated investigation stops here. No further feedback candidates or parameter variants were run after this screen. Production keeps its current 10% locomotion feedback only.

## One changed causal link

```text
p(L) = 1 - .50 * (1 - exp(-L/4))
lambda(node) = ln(2)/15 * p(L_tick_entry(node))
halfLife(node) = 15 / p(L_tick_entry(node)) seconds
```

**[Authored material semantics]** A materialized local column conditions the residence time of chemistry already in the environment. Bare nodes have native 15 s half-life; the bound approaches 30 s. This does not posit chemical storage inside a Plate, diffusion, sorption measurements, terrain height or access to buried layer identities. Public column count is a local conditioning proxy.

Each 316-grid field node reads its nearest 96-grid material coordinate. The wrapper pins tick-entry material before native decay. A same-tick later birth affects the next decay only. It saves pre-decay conditioned values, calls native decay once for bookkeeping and bare-node identity, then computes the declared single Float32 decay step for conditioned nodes. There is no correction multiplying already-rounded values upward.

Source secretion, Gaster accumulation/dab distance, retirement flushing, sensing/adherence, movement, gait, C writes, formation thresholds and audio are unchanged. Downstream differences can propagate through all of those native systems. After world divergence, later shared-RNG draws do not retain a common decision index.

## What is supported, and what remains open

- **[Measurement]** The changed decay law is valid and independent of secretion attenuation. Existing shared chemistry, subsequent traffic geography, B/E/F and material histories differ.
- **[Research interpretation]** Material can condition the chemical medium, rather than only how fast an ant crosses it or how much new field an ant writes. This is a genuinely different temporal responsibility.
- **[Unresolved]** The coupled screen has no mass-matched uniform-residence/global-gain world arm. Large field and material changes do not prove useful local material information. High native sensor saturation remains a competing explanation for the small aggregate adherence change.
- **[Unresolved]** One seed, rooted production Plate geometry, nearest-grid column lookup, capped Nodules and non-adopted max-D/X limit generality. Nothing demonstrates safe long-run accumulation beyond this 600 s case.
- **[Artistic/modeling choice]** Whether a material world should retain chemical traces longer is still a meaning choice. More layers are not a criterion for choosing it.

The next smallest closed question is **whether local material placement contributes recipient-level information beyond bulk retention**. A frozen-input, mass-matched spatial-control comparison can test that before another world campaign. It is proposed in the [coordinated synthesis](../2026-10-30-material-feedback-space/REPORT.md); it is not executed here. Production adoption/conformance is premature.
