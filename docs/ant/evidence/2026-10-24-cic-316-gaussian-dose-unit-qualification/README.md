# 316 Gaussian dose-unit qualification

## Question and decision

This fixed-cue pass asked whether Candidate F's remaining Gate 2 failures were materially due to carrying the 96-grid node-sum dose unit onto the refined 316 grid. Candidate G kept F's Gaussian geometry, stored-field format, reader, queries and sensor operators, and multiplied each requested write amount by the geometry-derived grid-area ratio.

**Decision: QUALIFIED FOR LATER MOVEMENT SHADOW.** G passed the existing Gate 2 checks, 16/16, including every route family, phase and zero-cue guard. This supports the bounded claim that correcting the interior reconstructed-integral unit removed F's registered fixed-cue Gate 2 blocker. It does not establish physical chemical units, natural movement improvement or production adoption. Gate 3 was not run.

## Candidate and unit result

F's field remains 316×316, with `h316=1/315`, production Gaussian `R=0.0045`, `σ=0.00225`, distance support `d≤R`, normalized Gaussian nodal weights, nonnegative Float32 storage, cap 8 and the unchanged bilinear reader. G changes only each frozen requested amount `A` to `A×scale`, where:

```text
scale = (h96 / h316)^2 = (315 / 95)^2 = 3969 / 361
      = 10.994459833795014
```

For interior nodes, the exact piecewise-bilinear integral is the tensor-product trapezoid sum times grid area. With no boundary nodes in these fixtures, G's expected integral is `sum(original requested doses)×h96²`, the legacy 96-grid convention. The observed maximum G-to-target integral residual across the 24 route/phase arms was `5.765245323242213e-13`; the maximum C-versus-G integral difference was `6.551430947222825e-12`. The largest G raster's node value was `0.1287035197019577`, far below cap 8.

The audit replayed 1,848 writes over all 24 arms. No write reached cap 8: 0 cap-hit write/nodes, 0 capped nodes, and 0 clamp loss. Requested and API-accepted scaled dose both totalled `546.2009063213192`; actual Float32 node sum was `546.2009063716978`, a `5.03786e-8` storage/accumulation residual. All F rasters regenerated byte-for-byte. F and G had identical active-node support. The maximum per-node `G − scale×F` residual was `1.2019478870528566e-8`, within the derived accumulation-rounding bound (maximum bound ratio `0.8163`); maximum centroid change was `1.0154280749574756e-9`. Gaussian weights and support were unchanged. Effective per-write `increment/dose` fractions differed at `5,207/11,523` node updates only because of floating operation order; the maximum fraction difference was `1.1102230246251565e-16`.

The maximum positive write-node distance was `0.004499367301607241≤R`. Writes used 4–8 nodes (200, 65, 686, 894 and 3 writes respectively). All qualified fixture support stayed in the interior. At boundaries, bilinear basis integrals are half/quarter sized for edge/corner nodes, so this area-ratio argument is not claimed as global boundary mass conservation.

## Claim boundary and next step

**Supported:** the fixed geometric area factor restores G's interior bilinear integral to the 96-grid node-sum convention within derived Float32 error, and G passes the existing fixed-cue Gate 2 checks. This makes the unscaled refined-grid unit a sufficient explanation for F's remaining tight/tightMirror MAE blockers in this assay.

**Not established:** a physical chemical-mass unit, boundary conservation, long-run cap behavior, other consumers' threshold/gain semantics, or improved ant movement. The unchanged nonlinear consumers respond to G's larger amplitude; the small increase in wide errors versus F and larger zero-cue magnitudes versus F remain relevant.

The smallest justified next step is a separately preregistered movement shadow that compares the same frozen world inputs under F and G, audits cap/threshold behavior during that shadow, and keeps production unchanged. No movement test or adoption occurred here.
